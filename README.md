# Early Warning Signals (EWS): 6-Month-Ahead Default Prediction

**Question the model answers:** *as of today, based on everything we know about a company up to today,
will it default in the next 6 months?*

A time-series ML model (gradient-boosted decision trees on point-in-time lag / rolling / trend features)
scores every active company each month. Success is measured with **Precision, Recall and Fire Rate**
(share of the portfolio flagged for review). The business target here is **recall of about 80%**: catch
roughly 4 in 5 companies that go on to default.

> **Data note:** all data is **synthetic (mock)**, generated by `src/generate_data.py`. No real company data is
> used. The mock design was iterated on (default-rate calibration, company-specific baselines) while building
> this project, so the numbers below illustrate the *method* and are not production performance.

## Approach

| Step | File | What it does |
|------|------|--------------|
| 1. Mock panel | `src/generate_data.py` | 3,000 companies x monthly snapshots (Jan-2022 to Aug-2026, ~152k rows). A hidden distress state drives 7 monthly signals (payment delays, delinquent invoices, GST filing delay, credit utilisation, adverse media, litigation, director changes) and 5 quarterly financial ratios published with a 1-month lag. Feed gaps and missing reports included. Defaulted companies stop reporting. |
| 2. Label | `make_label()` | `1` if the company defaults in months t+1 ... t+6, else `0`. Rows whose 6-month window is not fully observed are excluded, not treated as negatives. |
| 3. Features | `src/features.py` | 80 **point-in-time** features (only data <= t): current values, 3/6-month rolling mean, max and std, 3-month change, 6-month trend slope, deviation from the company's own baseline, red-flag count and its 6-month average, months of payment stress, financials staleness. |
| 4. Time-based split | `src/train_evaluate.py` | Train Jul-2022 to Mar-2024, validation Oct-2024 to Mar-2025, test Oct-2025 to Feb-2026, with **6-month embargo gaps** so no label window overlaps between sets. |
| 5. Tuning | same | `RandomizedSearchCV` (20 candidates) with **walk-forward (expanding-window) CV** and a 6-month gap. Objective: the **lowest fire rate needed to reach 80% recall**. |
| 6. Thresholds | same | Two alert thresholds fixed on the validation period and applied unchanged to test: a **watch-list threshold** giving 80% recall, and a **high-priority threshold** flagging the top 10% of scores. |
| 7. Evaluation | same | Precision, recall, fire rate, lift, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC; precision/recall vs fire rate; month-by-month stability; company-level recall and lead time; baselines. |
| 8. Scoring "today" | `src/predict.py` | Scores the latest snapshot and assigns High priority / Watch / Not flagged. |

Tuned hyper-parameters: `learning_rate=0.03, max_depth=4, max_leaf_nodes=15, min_samples_leaf=20, l2_regularization=0, max_iter=200, class_weight=None`.

## Results (test period Oct-2025 to Feb-2026: 12,499 company-months, 272 defaults in the next 6 months, base rate 2.18%)

**Watch-list tier (threshold set on validation for 80% recall):**

| Model | Recall | Precision | Fire rate | Lift | PR-AUC | ROC-AUC |
|-------|:------:|:---------:|:---------:|:----:|:------:|:-------:|
| Decision Tree (baseline) | 72.8% | 2.9% | 54.4% | 1.3x | 0.043 | 0.626 |
| GBDT, snapshot only (no history) | 80.2% | 3.3% | 53.0% | 1.5x | 0.065 | 0.722 |
| GBDT, default parameters | 79.8% | 3.5% | 50.1% | 1.6x | 0.055 | 0.729 |
| **GBDT, time-series features, tuned (final)** | **79.4%** | **3.5%** | **50.0%** | **1.6x** | **0.060** | **0.721** |

**High-priority tier (top 10% of scores), final model:** precision 7.2%, recall 32.7%, fire rate 9.9%, lift 3.3x.

Precision / recall trade-off by fire rate (final model, ranked by score):

| Fire rate | Precision | Recall | Lift |
|:--------:|:--------:|:------:|:----:|
| 2% | 9.2% | 8.5% | 4.2x |
| 5% | 8.5% | 19.5% | 3.9x |
| 10% | 7.1% | 32.7% | 3.3x |
| 20% | 5.2% | 48.2% | 2.4x |
| 30% | 4.5% | 62.5% | 2.1x |
| 40% | 3.9% | 71.0% | 1.8x |
| **50%** | **3.5%** | **79.4%** | **1.6x** |
| 60% | 3.1% | 85.7% | 1.4x |

**Read this honestly:**
- **Recall of ~80% costs a ~50% fire rate.** The ranking quality of the model (ROC-AUC 0.72) is modest, so reaching 80% recall means flagging about half of all companies, and only about 3.5% of those flagged default (1.6x better than random). The 80% recall comes from lowering the alert threshold, not from a more accurate model.
- **Model variants perform alike.** Tuned, default and snapshot-only models land within a few points of each other on every metric. With ~270 test defaults these gaps are within noise, so the value of history features and tuning should be re-tested on real data. All GBDT variants clearly beat the Decision Tree baseline.
- **Two tiers make it usable:** analysts work the High-priority tier first (7.2% precision, 3.3x lift), and the wider Watch tier gives the 80% recall safety net.

Other checks:
- **Stability across the five test months:** recall 77.6-80.8%, precision 2.9-4.1%, fire rate 49.5-50.6% (about 50 defaults per month, so month-level figures are noisy).
- **Company level:** 88 of 101 companies that defaulted after the test snapshots (87.1%) were flagged at least once, with a median lead time of 5 months.
- **Top drivers** (permutation importance): payment-delay level, GST-filing delay (3- and 6-month means), red-flag history, industry, credit utilisation.

## Limitations and next steps

- Synthetic data: labels come from a simulated hazard, so results do not reflect real default behaviour.
- To reach 80% recall at a much lower fire rate, the ranking itself has to improve: richer signals (bureau, bank transactions), more history, and calibrated probabilities on real data.
- Set thresholds from analyst capacity and the cost of a missed default; add SHAP explanations per flagged company; monitor drift and monthly recall / fire rate; refit on train + validation with thresholds re-derived.
